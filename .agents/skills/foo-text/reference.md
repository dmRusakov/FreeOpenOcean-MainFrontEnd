# FooText Technical Reference & Lexicon

This reference provides the specialized terminology, technical benchmarks, and engineering standards used when generating content in the FooText style.

---

## 1. Naval Architecture & Hull Dynamics

- **Waterline Length (LWL)**: The single most effective variable for reducing pitching moment, increasing hull speed, and enhancing directional stability in following seas. A longer waterline yields a lower Froude number at passage speeds.
- **Displacement/Length Ratio (D/L)**: Calculated as \( \text{Displacement (long tons)} / (0.01 \times \text{LWL in feet})^3 \). FooText favors moderate to light D/L ratios (under 120–160), allowing the hull to surf cleanly rather than bulldoze through boarding waves.
- **Prismatic Coefficient (\(C_p\))**: Measure of volume distribution along the hull. A high \(C_p\) (fuller ends, fine waterline entry with reserve buoyancy above) prevents pitchpoling and reduces dynamic pitching at sea.
- **Directional Stability vs. Maneuverability**: High directional tracking in quartering or following seas is paramount. Deep forefoots that grab and induce broaching are rejected; fine V-shaped entries that transition into a clean, flatter run aft to a canoe or narrow transom are preferred.
- **Structural Integrity & Scantlings**:
  - Heavy-gauge marine-grade 5083/5086 aluminum plate or monolithic vacuum-infused composite hulls.
  - Forward collision bulkhead positioned at 5% to 8% of LWL from the stem, built watertight to the deckhead without penetrations below the static waterline.
  - Watertight engine room bulkheads isolating machinery spaces from living quarters.
  - Rudder stocks engineered with a safety factor of 3x or higher over maximum hydrodynamic stall loads.
  - Standpipes / sea chests: Consolidating multiple through-hull penetrations into a single oversized, heavy-walled sea chest situated above the loaded waterline or with high-integrity isolation valves.

## 2. Heavy Weather Tactics & Gear Engineering

- **Active Running Off**:
  - Utilizing vessel speed and clean hull release to stay ahead of breaking wave crests.
  - Maintaining minimum steerageway (typically 6–9+ knots) to ensure rudder authority prevents broadside yawing.
  - Using autopilot with high-speed hydraulic ram or dedicated mechanical windvane backed by low-friction steering quadrant.
- **Passive Tactics & Drag Devices**:
  - **Series Drogue**: 100+ small fabric cones spliced into an elongated tapered nylon rode, attached to heavy transom chainplates via a balanced two-leg bridle. The gold standard for preventing pitchpoling in survival conditions.
  - **Speed-Limiting Drogue**: Single small drogue deployed astern to govern surfing speed in 35–50 knots, keeping hull speed below the broaching threshold while maintaining steerageway.
  - **Parachute Sea Anchor**: Deployed from the bow in deep water when running off is precluded by a lee shore. Requires a heavy nylon tether (minimum 10x to 15x vessel length) and an adjustable snubber to time vessel heave to the wave cycle.
- **Chafe Protection**: The silent killer of storm ground tackle and drogues.
  - Heavy leather, ballistic Cordura, or thick-walled vinyl hose seized tightly over rodes where passing through fairleads.
  - Dedicated heavy-duty bow and stern chocks with generous bend radii (minimum 6x to 8x the line diameter).

## 3. Ground Tackle & Anchoring Physics

- **Anchor Sizing Philosophy**: Always size ground tackle for a 60-knot squall in poor holding ground, not a 15-knot calm night in a sheltered basin. Upsize primary anchors by 1.5x to 2x standard classification society minimums (e.g., 100–150 lb scoop-type modern anchor on a 50–60 ft vessel).
- **Rode Composition**:
  - 100% High-Tensile G40 or G70 chain. Weight of chain provides catenary in light-to-moderate air and abrasion resistance on coral or sharp bedrock.
  - Chain stopper mounted securely to structural deck reinforcement forward of the windlass to eliminate shock loads on the windlass gypsy and shaft.
- **Snubber Geometry**:
  - Long 3-strand or dynamic double-braid nylon snubber (minimum 25–40 feet) attached to chain with an engineered chain hook or rolling hitch.
  - Two-leg V-bridle to distribute loads across both bow cleats and stabilize yawing at anchor.
  - Target minimum 10% elasticity under working load to dissipate kinetic surge forces.

## 4. Shipboard DC Electrical & Energy Independence

- **Energy Balance Metric**: Daily amp-hour consumption versus non-genset replenishment capability.
- **High-Output Engine Alternators**: Dual heavy-frame (large-case) alternators (150A–250A+ each) with external smart regulators configured with battery temperature sensors and remote field cutoffs.
- **Battery Chemistries**:
  - High-capacity LiFePO4 or heavy AGM banks capable of handling high continuous charge acceptance rates without thermal runaway.
  - Sized for a minimum of 48–72 hours of complete autonomous operation (refrigeration, radar, navigation instruments, continuous autopilot draw) without engine run time.
- **Solar & Passive Charging**: Hardtop-integrated high-efficiency monocrystalline solar panels wired through MPPT controllers with dedicated circuit isolation.

## 5. Marine Propulsion & Systems Reliability

- **Tandem Fuel Filtration**: Dual switchable Racor filters with differential vacuum gauges. Allows swapping filters on a running engine in under 15 seconds during a seaway.
- **Fuel Polishing**: Independent high-flow 12V/24V pump circulating tank contents through a 2-micron coalescing water-separator filter before fuel reaches the engine supply line.
- **Gravity-Feed Day Tank**: Clean, pre-filtered fuel pumped to an elevated day tank providing a positive head to the injection pump, eliminating aeration issues and air-leak vulnerabilities.
- **High-Capacity Dewatering**:
  - Primary automatic submersible bilge pump (500–1500 GPH) for nuisance water.
  - Secondary high-water emergency pump (3500–4000 GPH) mounted 12 inches above the bilge sump, wired through a dedicated alarm circuit.
  - Mechanical engine-driven crash pump or heavy-duty portable gasoline/diesel trash pump with reinforced suction hose for breach dewatering.

## 6. Practical Meteorology for the Ocean Voyager

- **500-Millibar (mb) Upper-Air Charts**:
  - The engine room of the atmosphere: surface weather reflects upper-level dynamics.
  - 5640m contour: the classic winter storm track benchmark in mid-latitudes.
  - Short waves and vorticity maxes on the 500mb chart trigger rapid explosive surface cyclogenesis (bombing lows).
- **Surface Pressure Tendency**: A barometric drop of 3 to 5 millibars in a 3-hour period is an unequivocal warning to prepare for severe gale conditions; 6+ mbar in 3 hours indicates storm-force or hurricane-force squalls imminent.
- **Current vs. Wind Shear**: Never underestimate wind against current (e.g., Agulhas, Gulf Stream, North Sea tidal races). Opposing 30-knot winds can shorten wave period by half and double wave steepness, transforming manageable 10-foot swells into vertical breaking walls capable of structural destruction.
