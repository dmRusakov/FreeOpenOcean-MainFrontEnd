About FreeOpenOcean

Engineered on the US Atlantic Coast by offshore sailors—building an open marine information portal, bathymetry map, and free ocean data infrastructure for the global voyaging community.

FreeOpenOcean is an independent maritime software initiative engineered along the Atlantic Coast of the United States. We operate as a lean, focused engineering group with zero corporate bureaucracy, prioritizing structural reliability, clean software architecture, and real-world utility over commercial marketing.

Mission & Sailor-First Philosophy

Our core doctrine is simple: ocean data is a vital safety asset, not a luxury commodity. When a vessel is underway offshore, access to accurate sounding data, ocean currents, and dynamic atmospheric pressure gradients directly dictates crew safety, hull preservation, and navigational survival. Closed proprietary ecosystems that lock public bathymetry behind subscriptions, obscure algorithmic assumptions, or charge exorbitant fees for crowdsourced tracks run counter to the foundational ethics of seamanship.

We build specifically for the realities of short-handed ocean sailing: fatigued two-person watches, rapid night sail changes, adverse sea states, and the need for immediate, high-contrast clarity on navigation displays. Every interface decision, vector tile optimization, and data layer we deploy is evaluated through one lens: does this improve situational awareness and safety under real passage conditions at sea?

Built by Sailors, for Sailors

First and foremost, we are active offshore sailors. We know firsthand the physical realities of passage-making, shifting weather windows, and the unforgiving nature of a lee shore. Safe navigation demands vast amounts of high-resolution environmental and hydrographic data. Yet today's commercial marine navigation systems remain largely locked behind closed proprietary formats, outdated user interfaces, and exorbitant paywalls. What makes this untenable is that much of this commercial cartography is built directly on publicly funded hydrographic data or derived from the very sounding logs and crowd tracks that private voyagers contribute every day. Our mission is straightforward: dismantle that closed ecosystem and provide the worldwide sailing community with maximum access to critical ocean data—openly, reliably, and completely free of charge.

The Ocean Marine Information Portal

To achieve this, we are engineering the Ocean Marine Information Portal, centered around the FreeOpenOcean Map. The platform consolidates high-resolution bathymetric soundings, comprehensive oceanographic metrics, and dynamic atmospheric forecasting into an agile, responsive interface—engineered with a clear roadmap toward direct, bidirectional connectivity with your vessel's onboard telemetry and NMEA networks. We are a tight-knit team of professional software engineers who build robust systems rapidly and to high performance tolerances. While FreeOpenOcean is not our commercial day job, it is our shared maritime commitment: a passion project developed to the exact same uncompromising standards we demand of our own shipboard hardware.

Open Ocean Data: Contribute and Collaborate

Accurate hydrography saves vessels. If you possess ocean data—whether private depth soundings, recorded passage tracks, local sounding corrections, or institutional archives—that you are willing to contribute freely so fellow mariners can navigate safely whenever they need it offshore, we stand ready to ingest, validate, and serve it. We welcome collaboration from government hydrographic agencies, private enterprises, ocean research groups, and individual voyagers. Attribution is entirely up to you: we can credit your contribution publicly across the platform, or keep it strictly confidential. For significant data contributions, we are eager to document and spotlight your support so the entire sailing community understands who made their waters safer.

Infrastructure, Investment, and Support

Building and maintaining high-availability geospatial marine infrastructure is an intensive engineering undertaking. We have already self-funded more than $80,000 to bring the system to life, and our recurring monthly operational expenditures continue to scale as we expand high-throughput cloud compute, multi-terabyte data storage, global tile distribution, and active AI model development. We welcome all donations and sponsorships to keep this infrastructure running and expanding. Contributions may be publicly acknowledged or held strictly private according to your preference. Every dollar goes directly into server capacity, data ingestion pipelines, and software development.

Formal Marine Navigation Disclaimer

Notice to Mariners & Legal Responsibility: FreeOpenOcean is an informational oceanographic platform and navigational decision-support aid. It does not replace official government hydrographic office carriage requirements (such as SOLAS, NOAA, UKHO, or CHS mandates) or certified Electronic Chart Display and Information Systems (ECDIS). Under international maritime law and standard rules of prudent seamanship (including COLREGS Rule 5 for proper lookout and Rule 7 for risk of collision), the master or skipper of the vessel maintains sole, non-delegable legal responsibility for the safe navigation, passage planning, grounding prevention, and command of the vessel.

Bathymetric soundings, shoaling channels, atmospheric frontal dynamics, and ocean current boundaries are constantly evolving. Never rely on any single electronic display or map layer as your sole source of navigational truth. Mariners must continuously cross-verify positions and depths against official nautical charts, Local Notices to Mariners, active radar, visual bearings, tide tables, and real-time echo sounders.
