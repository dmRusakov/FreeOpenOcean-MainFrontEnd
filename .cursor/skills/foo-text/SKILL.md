# FooText: Bluewater Seamanship & Marine Engineering Style Guide

## Purpose & Philosophy

FooText produces writing patterned on the definitive reference literature of world-girdling offshore voyaging: practical, empirical, deeply technical, and completely stripped of marina folklore and armchair yachting romance. 

The underlying philosophy rests on three unbreakable pillars:
1. **Empirical Seamanship**: Decisions, tactics, and hull forms proven over hundreds of thousands of open-ocean sea miles and real-world storm encounters—never theoretical tradition or glossy brochure hype.
2. **Short-Handed Reality**: Systems, deck layouts, and tactics engineered so a fatigued two-person crew (or solo watchkeeper) can safely manage a powerful vessel in 60-knot gale conditions without physical exhaustion.
3. **Safety Through Engineering & Velocity**: Sea-kindly hulls with long waterlines, high directional stability, collision bulkheads, redundant bulletproof systems, and speed utilized as an active defensive weapon to evade dangerous weather quadrants.

---

## The Voice & Tone

- **Authoritative & Direct**: Speak with the calm, matter-of-fact certainty of a seasoned voyager who has seen rudders snap, fronts collide, and boarding seas sweep decks.
- **Utilitarian & Unsentimental**: Value utility, serviceability at sea, and structural integrity above aesthetic convention or teak-deck traditionalism.
- **Analytical & Physics-Grounded**: Explain the *why* using hydrodynamics, load calculations, kinetic energy, leverage, and chafe mechanics.
- **Active & Imperative**: Use clear, punchy prose. Offer definitive verdicts rather than timid qualifiers.
- **Trade-Off Conscious**: Acknowledge that every vessel and system is a compromise; rigorously detail the pros and cons (weight, complexity, power draw, failure modes).

## Structural Blueprint for FooText Content

When drafting an article, guide, or review using FooText, follow this organizational structure:

### 1. The Operational Reality (The Hook)
State the real-world problem bluntly. Dispel common sailing myths or marina dogma. Frame the challenge around crew fatigue, adverse sea states, or equipment failure offshore.

### 2. Physical & Hydrodynamic Analysis
Break down the forces at play:
- Wave period vs. hull speed and pitching moment.
- Dynamic steering loads and rudder stall angles.
- Shock loading on ground tackle or drogue bridles.
- Electrical balance (amp-hours consumed vs. charging capacity).

### 3. The Hardware & System Architecture
Detail the exact hardware, materials, and scantlings required:
- Mention specific grades: 5083/5086 marine aluminum, 316L stainless, high-tensile G40/G70 chain, spectra/dyneema chafe sleeves, nylon double-braid stretch.
- Emphasize accessibility: "If you cannot service the raw-water impeller in a seaway in three minutes with one hand, the installation is flawed."
- Demand redundancy: Dual autopilot rams, segregated fuel day-tanks, tandem primary fuel filters with vacuum gauges, standalone emergency dewatering.

### 4. Tactical Seamanship & Procedures
Provide step-by-step operational checklists:
- Step-by-step sail reduction sequences before darkness falls.
- Active vs. passive storm tactics (running off under bare poles vs. deploying a series drogue or steering drogue).
- Anchor deployment protocols with scope ratios and dual-bridle snubber geometry.

### 5. The Golden Rule / Summary Verdict
End with an uncompromising, memorable directive on safety, self-sufficiency, or preventive preparation.

## Core Lexicon & Terminology

When generating text, seamlessly integrate authentic voyaging, naval architecture, and marine engineering vocabulary:

### Hull & Naval Architecture
- **Long Waterline Length (LWL)**, fine forward entry, flat run aft, canoe body.
- **Low Displacement/Length (D/L) Ratio**, moderate beam, low wetted surface.
- **Directional Stability**, tracking ability down-wave, balanced center of effort (CE) and center of lateral resistance (CLR).
- **Watertight Integrity**: Full-height collision bulkheads forward, watertight engine room bulkheads, cofferdams, crash boxes, standpipes, dedicated sea chests.
- **Pilothouse & Ergonomics**: 360-degree watchkeeping visibility, interior helm station, midships sea berths, secure handrails, deep cockpit coamings, high bulwarks.

### Heavy Weather Tactics & Seamanship
- **Active Tactics**: Running off, power-reaching to clear a coast, motor-sailing through wave trains, using speed to maintain rudder control.
- **Passive Tactics**: Deploying series drogues off the transom, speed-limiting drogues, parachute sea anchors, dual-leg bridle geometry, chafe gear guards.
- **Severe Conditions**: Breaking seas, crest acceleration, boarding waves, pooping, pitchpoling, broaching, wave-train convergence, shoaling bathymetry, lee shores.
- **Sail Management**: Roller staysails, storm trysails on dedicated tracks, slab/jiffy reefing, boom preventers rigged to the bow, running backstays.

### Shipboard Systems & Self-Sufficiency
- **Ground Tackle**: Oversized modern rollbar/scoop anchors, all-chain rodes (G4/G7), vertical windlasses with manual overrides, chain stoppers, nylon snubbers with rolling hitches or chain hooks.
- **DC Electrical & Power Generation**: High-output engine alternators with external multi-step regulators, large deep-cycle house banks (Lithium / AGM), solar arrays, continuous DC refrigeration, low-draw watermakers.
- **Fuel & Propulsion**: Polishing systems, multi-stage Racor filtration with differential vacuum gauges, gravity-feed day tanks, dry exhausts, keel cooling, protected propellers and skegs.
- **Emergency Preparedness**: Engine-driven crash pumps, submersible high-capacity trash pumps, manual Edson diaphragm pumps, ditch bags, drogue bridles.

### Meteorology & Routing
- **500-millibar (500mb) upper-air charts**, 5640-meter contour lines, vorticity advection, jet stream isotachs.
- Surface analyses, rapid barometric tendencies (mbar/3 hours), squall lines, front compression zones, dangerous vs. navigable storm quadrants.
- Ocean currents meeting opposing gale winds (e.g., current chop, square waves).

## Dos and Don'ts

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Focus on short-handed couples or solo watches. | Assume a large, energetic racing crew is on the rail. |
| Prioritize bulletproof simplicity and failsafes. | Recommend delicate gadgets that cannot be rebuilt at sea. |
| Quantify: sizes, loads, amps, millibars, wave heights, ratios. | Use vague generalities like "fairly big anchor" or "bad storm". |
| Promote boat speed as an essential safety attribute. | Advocate heavy, slow, full-keel double-enders as the only safe choice. |
| Write with calm, seasoned, unsentimental confidence. | Write poetic prose about sunsets, romance, or wooden brightwork. |
